We're teaching the origin and coinage of the term "persuasive computing" to help change behavior. Perhaps the most successful behavior change campaign so far has been social media sites and apps capturing our attention, essentially persuading you to stay on them. HCI researchers are busy trying to understand how that works, for example Zannettou et al.: [Analyzing User Engagement with TikTok’s Short Format Video Recommendations using Data Donations](https://homes.cs.washington.edu/~franzi/pdf/zannettou-tiktok-chi24.pdf). An interview with the author includes this candid response: 

> "I was also a little surprised that people watch only about 55% of videos to the end. We debated whether this was high or low. Is this part of the platform’s design, that once you’ve got whatever you wanted to get out of this video you move on? Or is it a sign that even this highly tuned recommendation algorithm is not doing that well? I don’t know which it is."

Finally, at the start of the module I showed a video by the Center for Humane Technology, where they talked about how humanity has "failed" in its first contact with AI. If you watch it all the way to the end, you'll see that one of their largest concerns is an AI's ability to persuade people. You can see the video [here](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xoVJKj8lcNQ).
